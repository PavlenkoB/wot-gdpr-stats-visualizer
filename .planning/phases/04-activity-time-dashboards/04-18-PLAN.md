At 375px two tiles share the 293px card, so a tile's inner width is 104.5px; "2,467" plus a 4px gap plus "hours" needs 118.9px. A numeral and its unit are a formatted number and a fixed word, not untrusted text; the anywhere rule was inherited from the UI-SPEC's blanket "every flex or grid child that can hold untrusted text" sentence and applied to the one child that holds none. Prototyped in the built app by setting the three properties below through the CSSOM: the numeral and the unit each measure on one line at both widths, the unit drops under the numeral at 375px (tile 114.8px high), and nothing changes at 1280px.

2. G-04-UI-3 (legend). ChartLegend is a wrapping flex row of the word Less, the swatches, the word More and the note, with a 4px gap in both directions. ## Decisions recorded for the reader (the user should see these)

- Tile layout at 375px keeps two tiles per row and stacks the unit under a four-digit numeral, instead of raising the tile minimum to force one tile per row. The audit offered both; stacking keeps the UI-SPEC's two-per-row contract and does not lengthen the summary by a whole tile row.
- The tile numerals no longer carry overflow-wrap: anywhere. A numeral wider than a tile's inner width (seven digits with separators, over a million sessions) would now overflow its padding instead of breaking mid-number. That is accepted: realistic exports are far below it (the scale target is 60,000 sessions and a six-character numeral measures about 92px against 104.5px), and a number broken across lines is a wrong reading of a figure, not a degraded one.
- The legend change is stylesheet-only on the shared ChartLegend, so it applies to both heatmaps. The Phase 5 FlowLegend is a separate copy and is deliberately left as it is.
- Not addressed, as instructed: touch scroll affordance, tooltips on touch, the level-0 hairline, nav clipping, and desktop chart width against the card.
