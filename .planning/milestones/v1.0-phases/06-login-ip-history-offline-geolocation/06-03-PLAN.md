  <decision>When a dataset has more distinct addresses than MAX_LOGIN_IPS (20,000), do the logins of the addresses that are not listed still count in the country figures and on the map?</decision>
  <context>UI-SPEC UI Considerations, the one unresolved row. The cap exists only to bound the stored record against a hostile archive. A country tally has at most about 250 entries, so keeping capped logins in the country figures costs no memory. The UI-SPEC requires an honest line, "{m} more IP addresses ({k} logins) are counted in the totals but not listed in the table", and requires that drawn countries plus not-drawn countries plus unknown-country logins equal the tile total. That sum only holds when capped logins are tallied. Recommended: option-a.</context>
  <options>
    <option id="option-a">
      <name>Capped logins still reach the country figures, tiles and map (recommended)</name>
      <pros>The tile total equals the sum over the map, the not-drawn list and the unknown row at any size; the cap bounds memory, not the truth; nothing silently undercounts.</pros>
      <cons>The table rows then sum to less than the stated total for an export over the cap, which the cap line states in words.</cons>
    </option>

      <pros>The table rows always sum to the stated totals.</pros>
      <cons>The map and the Top country tile silently undercount for a large export; the UI-SPEC sum invariant breaks; the total on the tile and the sum on the map disagree.</cons>
    </option>

Keep every 06-01 export and behaviour, and extend `parseIp` to accept IPv6 inside the same function: after the length bound and trim, route on the presence of a colon. For IPv6 accept RFC 4291 text: up to eight groups of one to four hex digits, one `::` compression, either letter case, and an embedded dotted IPv4 tail in the last 32 bits (the tail follows the strict IPv4 rules, so a padded group in it is rejected). Reject a `%` zone id, a bracketed form, a triple colon, a second compression, more than eight groups, a group over four hex digits, an empty group other than inside the compression, and any character outside hex, colon and dot. Parse with a single left-to-right scan or anchored patterns with no nested quantifiers; never a backtracking-prone alternation. The canonical form for IPv6 is eight lower-case groups, no leading zeros, no compression, and any dotted tail converted to its two hex groups (`::ffff:192.0.2.1` becomes `0:0:0:0:0:ffff:c000:201`). `bytes` is the 16-byte big-endian value.

Extend `maskIp` for IPv6: the first three groups of the canonical form followed by five `xxxx` groups (`2001:db8:0:xxxx:xxxx:xxxx:xxxx:xxxx`); an input that is neither canonical IPv4 nor canonical IPv6 still returns the fixed text `xxx`. Add `compareIp(a: ParsedIp, b: ParsedIp): number` (every IPv4 before every IPv6, then byte-wise ascending) and `mappedIpv4(ip: ParsedIp): ParsedIp | null` (for an IPv4-mapped IPv6 address, the equivalent IPv4 `ParsedIp`, otherwise null; the country lookup in plan 06-06 uses it). None of these ever throws or echoes an input.

In `login-aggregate.ts`, the only change this task needs is that the grouping key is the new canonical form, which it already reads from `parseIp`; confirm that an IPv6 row and its differently spelled twin land in ONE address, an IPv4-mapped spelling stays its own address (the UI-SPEC rule is one row per canonical address), and a hostile cell is counted unreadable.
